# Scoring Rubric: Product Designer

This challenge-specific rubric supplements the scoring methodology.

## Challenge-Specific Evaluation Points

### Strategic Depth (30 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Root cause analysis that goes past the obvious "team invites cause the drop-off" narrative
- Recognition that the funnel likely contains multiple independent failure modes, not one
- Honest engagement with the user research — including its limitations (sample size, selection bias)
- Trade-off awareness between immediate conversion lift and downstream retention/virality metrics
- Understanding of B2B buying psychology: why individual users need to validate before advocating internally

**Red flags:**
- "Just remove steps" without engaging with why those steps exist
- Treats the team-invite drop-off as the singular root cause
- Ignores the virality and retention guardrails in the brief
- Quotes user research as proof rather than signal
- Proposes solutions without naming what they'd want to learn before committing

### Specificity (25 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Wireframes or detailed flow descriptions at the right fidelity — hand-drawn or low-fi is fine, polish isn't the point
- Specific copy or microcopy for the moments that matter (skip-the-tour CTAs, sample-data banners, recovery prompts)
- Step-by-step flow with interaction-level detail, not just layout
- Measurable success criteria with defensible targets
- A V1 scope that names what's in *and* what's out, with reasoning

**Red flags:**
- "Improve the onboarding" or "streamline the flow" without specifics
- Wireframes that are only boxes with labels — no interaction states, no edge cases
- Pixel-perfect mockups with no design rationale (we asked for thinking, not portfolios)
- Vague success metrics like "improve engagement" or "drive activation"
- V1 scope that includes everything (no prioritization)

### AI Fluency (20 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- A real AI workflow from their own practice — research synthesis, copy generation, variant exploration, prototype generation, whatever they actually use
- Concrete before/after detail that demonstrates the workflow is real, not aspirational
- Honest read on where AI fails in design work and how they handle those failures
- AI as a multiplier on the work itself, not just as a feature inside the redesigned product

**Red flags:**
- "I use ChatGPT to brainstorm" with no specifics
- Anti-AI posture or skepticism at an AI-native company (instant disqualifier)
- Treating AI fluency only as "we should add AI features to the onboarding" — that's a product idea, not a workflow
- Generic AI tool list with no integration story
- All upside, no honest acknowledgment of limitations

### Design Judgment (15 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Tradeoffs named explicitly: "I chose X. The cost is Y. I accept that because Z."
- A clear point of view on what's out of scope for V1 and why
- Recognition of edge cases the brief doesn't surface: returning users, invited users, mobile signups, multi-workspace users
- Realistic engineering effort estimates that respect the 2-week sprint constraint
- Pattern recognition from consumer or B2B apps applied with judgment, not just imported

**Bonus signal:**
- Proposes a research plan to resolve the ambiguity in the user data
- Names a guardrail metric that would prove the redesign failed even if conversion improved
- Disagrees with one of the brief's framings (e.g., the 60% target, the user research interpretation) and defends the disagreement
- Treats template content as a separate workstream with its own deliverables — signals real shipping experience

### Communication (10 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Clear before/after comparison
- Visual artifacts at appropriate fidelity (any fidelity is fine; matching fidelity to question is what matters)
- Logical flow from problem framing to solution to scope
- Respects the 4-page limit and the 1-2 hour time guidance
- Direct, decision-oriented language — operator voice, not consultant voice

**Red flags:**
- Wall-of-text without visual structure
- Over-polished portfolio pieces that bury the thinking
- Exceeds the page limit (signals inability to prioritize)
- Buzzword-heavy prose without substance

## Hints for What Beats Claude

Claude's baseline answer is competent and structured:
- Identifies multiple failure modes instead of collapsing them into one narrative
- Acknowledges user research limitations honestly
- Includes a recovery layer for deferred steps
- Names edge cases that V1 doesn't address

**To beat it, you might:**
- Disagree with Claude's prioritization — argue that a different failure mode is the V1 priority and defend it
- Propose a specific research plan to resolve the ambiguity in the exit interviews before scaling the redesign
- Go deeper on one of the edge cases Claude flags but defers — returning users, invited users, mobile, multi-workspace
- Include actual microcopy for the moments that matter (sample-data banner, skip-the-tour, recovery prompts) — most submissions skip this
- Propose an A/B testing strategy that protects the guardrail metrics
- Show how you'd measure the "aha moment" qualitatively, not just as a conversion event
- Take a sharper position on the conversion target — argue for a specific number and defend it with evidence from comparable B2B onboarding rebuilds you've shipped
- Share a real story of an onboarding redesign you shipped — what broke, what you learned, what you'd do differently

**Claude's answer tends to be:**
- Structurally sound but missing the specific texture of someone who's shipped onboarding before
- Honest about limitations but generous about what it defers
- Correct on principles ("show don't tell," "progressive disclosure") without showing the earned intuition for when to apply them
- Strong on framing, weaker on the microcopy and edge cases that real designers obsess over

The bar to beat is "good UX generalist who's read the canon." To win, show us you've actually shipped onboarding through a release cycle where the numbers didn't move and you had to figure out why.
